# 内容仕様書 — 鎌倉の刃 (Content Spec)
## Companion to the design brief. What goes on the site, in what words, and what deliberately does not.

---

## 0. How to use this

The design brief says *how the site looks and behaves.* This says *what content and terminology fills it.* Hand both to the build agent. Every Japanese term below is verified — **the agent must use these exact kanji and readings and must not invent blade names, process names, or anatomy terms.**

Guiding rule: **content is curated, not comprehensive.** Reference material was provided covering ~40 knife types, ~11 sword classes, a 12-step process, five regions, and adjacent crafts. Almost none of that appears verbatim. Depth is signalled by *confident selection*, not volume. See §10 for what is deliberately cut and why.

---

## 1. The atelier's identity (the honest anchor)

- **Tradition:** the atelier works in **相州伝 (Sōshū-den)** — the Sagami-province sword tradition centred on Kamakura, historically the peak school of Japanese swordsmithing. This ties the name 鎌倉の刃 to real history and gives the copy a specific point of view (deep 反り, vivid 刃文, robust construction).
- **You MAY claim:** the *tradition, method, region, and aesthetic* (相州伝, 鎌倉, deep curve, active hamon). These are schools/techniques, not personal pedigrees.
- **You must NOT claim:** descent from a named historical master (Masamune / 正宗 etc.), a specific founding year, imperial or shogunal endorsement, or 人間国宝 (Living National Treasure) status. Evoke the era; never forge a pedigree.
- **Register:** the atelier speaks of itself humbly (謙譲語) and of the visitor politely (丁寧語・御 honorifics). It never boasts.

## 3. 匠の技 — the craft (the spine)

The 12-step reference process is distilled to **six stages** — a genuine sequence, so numbering it is appropriate. Do not expand back to twelve micro-steps; that's a manual, not a page.

1. **たたら・玉鋼** — iron sand smelted in a one-use clay furnace over three days yields the steel bloom (鉧); the master sorts out the high-carbon 玉鋼.
2. **折り返し鍛錬** — the steel is heated, hammered, and folded back on itself repeatedly to expel impurities and even the carbon.
3. **造り込み** — hard edge-steel (鋼) and soft core-iron (地鉄) are forge-welded together (甲伏せ / 三枚) so the blade is hard yet not brittle.
4. **火造り** — the composite bar is drawn out and shaped; the point (切先) and bevel lines are hammered in.
5. **土置き・焼き入れ** — clay laid thick on the spine, thin on the edge; heated in darkness, judged by colour alone, and quenched. The edge freezes hard (刃文 appears); the differential sets the curve (反り). **← this is the one dark passage in the design (藍鉄).**
6. **研ぎ・拵** — a dedicated polisher (研師) reveals the grain and edge over many stones; a metalsmith fits the collar (鎺); the blade rests in a plain magnolia scabbard (白鞘) or dressed mounting (拵).

**The science, stated plainly (not as a physics lecture):** one or two lines at most — the edge becomes hard and keen while the spine stays supple enough to absorb a blow, and it is this difference, not decoration, that draws the 刃文 and bends the 反り. Keep the atmosphere; don't print a metallurgy paper.

## 4. The living lineage (the through-line)

The single most compelling, most authentic thread — feature it, don't bury it:

> When the wearing of swords was abolished in the Meiji era (**廃刀令**), the master smiths turned their sword-craft to the kitchen. The same lamination and clay-tempering that made the katana now make the world's finest knives — **牛刀 (gyūtō), 柳刃 (yanagiba), 出刃 (deba).**

This justifies the one culinary blade in the catalogue and gives the atelier a reason to exist *today*, not just as a relic. It is the answer to "why does a swordsmith have a website in 2026."

- 「なぜ、折り返すのか」 (why we fold the steel)
- 「忍者は直刀を使ったか」 (myth-busting, rewritten as a Japanese essay — not translated clickbait)

Framed as a smith's notes (手記), not a "blog." Long-form, Mincho body, vertical pull-quotes.

## 6. Philosophy — the words that carry the spirit

Use in 鍛冶場 / 匠の技, sparingly, never as marketing slogans:
- **職人 (shokunin)** — the craftsman bound by duty to make flawlessly.
- **ものづくり (monozukuri)** — reverence for material and detail in the act of making.
- **こだわり (kodawari)** — the uncompromising pursuit, whatever the cost in time.
- **研ぎは、心を研ぐ** — the polish sharpens the mind (togi as meditation) — as a pull-quote, not a claim.

## 9. Deliberately CUT / context-only (everything important is accounted for)

| Reference material | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Full ~40-item 庖丁 typology (蛸引, ふぐ引, 骨スキ, 麺切 …) | **CUT.** Keep only 柳刃/牛刀/出刃 as the lineage bridge | An atelier shows what it makes, not a catalogue. Forty knives kills the 間. |
| 大太刀・小太刀・長巻・直刀・剣・鉈 | **CUT** | Obscure variants dilute the core 刀/脇差/短刀/太刀 range. |
| Five regional traditions (関/堺/越前/燕三条/土佐) table | **CUT.** Anchor to 相州伝 only | One atelier has one lineage, not a survey of all of them. |
| Woodworking tools (鑿/鉋/鋸) | **CONTEXT ONLY** | One sentence: the smith also forges tools for other artisans. Not a section. |
| 漆 urushi / 和紙 washi / 江戸切子 / 組子 / 指物 | **CONTEXT ONLY** | The scabbard is lacquered by an urushi specialist — a line in 匠の技 about the network of hands. Not sections. |
| Full 12-step process, martensite/pearlite detail | **DISTILLED** to §3's six stages + two plain lines | The science serves atmosphere, not a materials lecture. |
